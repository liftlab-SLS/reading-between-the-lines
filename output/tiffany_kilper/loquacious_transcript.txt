A: A chargeback is essentially a rebate mechanism in the pharmaceutical distribution system. When we had contracted pricing with end customers, but those customers bought their products through distributors rather than directly from us, we would use the chargeback process to make sure the customer got their contracted price.

So here's how it worked - a distributor would purchase products from us at our wholesale acquisition cost, our list price basically. Then when that distributor sold the product to an end customer who had a contract with us for a lower price, the distributor would submit a chargeback to us for the difference between what they paid us and what the contracted customer price was supposed to be. We would then reimburse the distributor for that difference, which is the chargeback amount.

It was really important for managing our contract pricing and making sure customers got the pricing they were entitled to under their agreements with us, even when they were buying through the distribution chain rather than directly from Mallinckrodt. The chargeback group had to validate these claims, make sure the pricing was correct, verify that the customers were legitimate, and process the payments to the distributors. It was a pretty complex process with a lot of financial implications for the company.
